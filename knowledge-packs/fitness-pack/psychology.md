# Behavioral Psychology for Fitness

## Habit Formation

### How Habits Work
- Cue → Routine → Reward (the habit loop)
- Cue must be obvious
- Routine must be easy
- Reward must be satisfying

### Making Habits Stick
- Start smaller than you think you need
- "I will do 5 minutes of exercise" is better than "I will train for an hour"
- Habit stacking: "After I [existing habit], I will [new habit]"
- Example: "After I brush my teeth, I will put on my workout clothes"

### Identity-Based Habits
- Instead of "I want to lose weight" → "I am someone who exercises"
- Every workout is a vote for the identity
- Focus on the person you're becoming, not the result

## Motivation vs Discipline

### Motivation
- Unreliable, comes and goes
- Use it when it's there, don't rely on it
- Best for getting started on something new

### Discipline
- Reliable, can be built
- Only needed for the first few weeks
- After that, it's habit
- The 5-Minute Rule: commit to 5 minutes only

## Adherence Strategies

### The 80/20 Rule
- 80% consistency beats 100% intensity
- Missing one workout doesn't ruin progress
- Getting back on track is what matters

### Environment Design
- Make good habits easy (set out gym clothes night before)
- Make bad habits hard (don't buy junk food)
- Remove friction from exercise

### Social Accountability
- Training partner
- Telling someone your goal
- Public commitment
- Community support

## Setbacks and Relapse

### Expect Them
- 100% of people have setbacks
- Perfection is not the goal, consistency is
- One bad meal/day/week doesn't matter long-term

### The "Never Miss Twice" Rule
- Missing one session is fine
- Missing two in a row is a pattern
- Get back on track immediately

### Learning from Setbacks
- What triggered it? (stress, travel, sickness?)
- What can you change for next time?
- Adjust the plan, don't abandon it

## Goal Setting

### SMART Goals
- Specific: "Lose 5kg" not "get fit"
- Measurable: track with numbers
- Achievable: realistic timeframe
- Relevant: aligned with your values
- Time-bound: set a deadline

### Process vs Outcome Goals
- Outcome: "Lose 5kg" (less control)
- Process: "Train 3x this week" (full control)
- Focus on process goals — outcomes follow

### Goal Types
- Daily habits (non-negotiable, small)
- Weekly targets (3 sessions, 10k steps each day)
- Monthly milestones (progress check)
- Long-term vision (6-12 months)

## Common Psychological Barriers

### All-or-Nothing Thinking
- "I already ate one cookie, might as well eat the whole box"
- Solution: progress not perfection

### Comparison Trap
- Comparing Day 1 to someone else's Year 5
- Social media fitness accounts are highlights, not reality

### Immediate Gratification Bias
- Training doesn't show results for 4-6 weeks
- Need systems, not willpower
- Trust the process, measure weekly not daily
